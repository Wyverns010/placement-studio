Work through these steps for the ad, in order.

1. Find this persona's motivation: read its description, messaging preferences and what it is disinterested in, and name the one need or desire that would make this person stop scrolling, in their own terms.
2. Open with that motivation. The headline speaks to what this persona wants, as a benefit to them, not a description of the product or the business.
3. Then give one concrete, true fact from the facts above as the proof. Restate it in your own words; it serves the benefit, it is not the headline.
4. Close with the call to action: one standard ad button, exactly as written, from {ctas}. Pick the one that fits how this product is bought: "Subscribe now" or "Start subscription" for a subscription, "Book now" for a class or a service, "Get offer" only when the advertiser states an offer, otherwise "Shop now", "Buy now", "Add to cart" or "Order now". The headline and body carry the persona; the button stays plain.
5. Pick your angle. Each persona's ad in this campaign takes a different one: emotional (how it feels), practical (what it saves or fixes), or proof (a specific detail that shows it). Silently consider all three, choose the one that fits this persona best and differs from the other personas listed above, and write only that. Do not mention the angle in the output.
6. Write in your own words. Never copy the advertiser's sentences, or any phrase longer than about four words, verbatim: the advertiser text is a brief to you, not ad copy. Single facts and short product terms (for example "grain-free" or "hand-poured") may be reused. Code checks for copied runs of words and sends the ad back.
